What is real here, and what to swap for production

Real: camera, on-device analysis, AR boxes, GPS tagging, offline queue and auto-sync. Stand-in: the analysis is a simple colour-based check, not a trained model. Replace analyzeCells() with a MobileNetV3 or EfficientNet-Lite model run through TensorFlow Lite (or TF.js in the browser) and point SYNC_URL at your server. Seeing stress before spots appear needs more than a normal phone camera: use a clip-on near-infrared sensor or a model trained on carefully labelled early-stage data.

What is real here, and what to swap for production

Real: browser speech-to-text and text-to-speech (works best in Chrome on Android), GPS, and the prescription layout. Stand-in: getContext() returns sample soil and rain data, and agent() is rule-based for wilting and yellow leaves. In production, call Bhashini or Whisper for speech, Open-Meteo or IMD for rain, the Soil Health Card for soil, and an LLM through LangChain for the answer. Have an agronomist approve all chemical doses.

What is real here, and what to swap for production

Real: a simulation that runs 2,000 possible seasons from price swings, yield changes and pest chance, plus a saved ledger. Stand-in: price history is sample data, forecast() is a simple trend, and the risk simulation is basic. In production, load Agmarknet history from a FastAPI backend, replace forecast() with Prophet or an LSTM, and use your pincode's climate and humidity forecast. Always show the model's accuracy and keep forecasts as guidance, not a promise.
